# iconalmas Cyber — Product Research

## 1. Our Mission

Build a genuinely useful cybersecurity product that helps organizations protect critical infrastructure and industrial systems.

We want to solve a real security problem, not build a generic dashboard simply to add AI.

## 2. Our Initial Product Hypothesis

Investigate whether we can help organizations understand:

- Which assets and vulnerabilities create the greatest risk.
- How weaknesses and connections between systems can combine into dangerous attack paths.
- Which remediation actions should be performed first.
- Why a finding matters to business operations.
- How to verify whether a security fix actually reduces risk.

AI may assist with investigation, explanations, and recommendations. Security-critical calculations must be evidence-based, testable, and appropriately validated.

## 3. Initial Research Finding

Existing products already offer OT asset visibility, vulnerability prioritization, remediation guidance, and attack-path analysis.

Examples to investigate:
- Tenable OT Security
- Rockwell Automation OT cybersecurity services
- Dragos industrial cybersecurity platform

We must understand their capabilities before deciding what to build.

## 4. Potential Market Gap — Not Yet Validated

Investigate whether smaller industrial organizations struggle to turn existing security-tool data into affordable, trustworthy, evidence-backed remediation decisions that account for operational impact.

This is a hypothesis, not a proven market gap.

## 5. Research Questions

1. Who experiences this problem?
2. How do they solve it today?
3. What do existing products already do well?
4. Where do current workflows remain difficult, expensive, or unreliable?
5. What evidence would demonstrate that our approach is better?
6. Would a customer adopt or pay for the solution?
7. What safety, privacy, and deployment requirements would apply?

## 6. Development Principles

- Research before building.
- Use AI to accelerate work, but verify its output.
- Prefer existing tools when they already solve a problem well.
- Start with a small, meaningful prototype.
- Test using synthetic data and authorized environments.
- Measure results and obtain practitioner feedback.
- Do not claim uniqueness or customer value without evidence.
